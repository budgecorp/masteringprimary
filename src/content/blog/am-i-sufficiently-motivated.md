I am primarily motivated by annoyance, frustration and shame. I like good ideas in the moment but they don't sustain me for very long.

I think what we envy about good farmers and carpenters is that they develop and then execute on a skill for a long time because they want to get great at that skill — not because they are afraid of failing. This might be the mindset of any good craftsperson but I doubt it. I think moment to moment they are working on doing something well, but the thing that moves them forward is a fear of not having enough food, their families starving, their crops or animals dying — and that over time that turns into discipline that keeps them motivated and looks like passion for excellence. I think this is also true for anyone working in the knowledge economy for an extended period of time.

So what happens when you intentionally decrease your anxiety, frustration, and anger in your day-to-day, without increasing your desire to achieve a positive outcome? I do less. I execute on what comes to me, and what I therefore perceive as urgent, without trying to accomplish a planned outcome. I don't move the needle forward.

I have a list of outcomes I've listed to accomplish but I don't pick them up — or, when I do, it is later in the day, once I've dealt with the noise, and I have limited energy. Counter-intuitive but I think the only option is to complete required work first thing in the morning, and then manage what shows up for the rest of the day.

I expect it is just like running, likely with the same energy boost. If I commit to doing it in the morning, every morning, no matter what else is going on, and get up very early if I need to, then I will progress. If I don't, then I will get older with the same aspirations, and eventually die very aware that I allied with my weakness.

Last night, lying in bed at 11:30, I was struck by a sense that if I didn't get in the cold tub, as I had committed, then I would never feel worthy. Ever. Somewhere in me a tiny voice, barely audible, pulled me out of bed with a sense of purpose and I got into the tub. This morning I ran early, with only a little resistance.

The last time I was running every morning I put my clothes out, my shoes at the door, and I went out before sitting on the couch to meditate or read the news. I'm sure I could do the same with work. The required document open, the game plan defined, the trail to get me to the desk (run, teeth, tea) undoubted.
