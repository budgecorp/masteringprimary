I've felt best when I have given everything I've got and can feel the result. My image of a well-lived life is a lean hard man with some deep smile lines, who has lived close enough to the line of life to deeply understand his failings and insignificance, but invigorated by the freedom this has allowed him.

It's difficult to picture that man in an office every day, but not impossible. In my mind, this man or woman would need to feel a deep respect for others — a respect that goes beyond the obvious and may require conscious effort. They would need to accept the failings of others, having learned to accept their own. They would need to be there so they can both engage in work of deep concentration and effort, and because they greatly value the impact of being and working among others who have different values, talents and shortcomings.

The trick would be to still produce — to move beyond work as an occupation and a practice, to where it is a compelling and encompassing purpose. It is here that ambition and need so often plays a role, often what seems like a necessary role.

The book Siddhartha tries to describe a working man who is still motivated by a higher power, but it fails in its lack of depth and unwillingness to be distracted from the spiritual theme. The leader-as-servant is a nice idea and may be practical; the team model presented by basketball coaching great Wooden seems more time-tested. I'll let you know if a focus on process, hard work and practice rather than winning is a winning combination.
