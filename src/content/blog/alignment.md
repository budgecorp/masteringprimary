Coming to the mat in a consistent manner is one of the few ways to align our minds with our bodies. Each day we are forced to recognize where our bodies and minds are at with sufficient clarity to be fully engaged.

If we do yoga when we want to we will naturally pick the times when our minds and our bodies are sufficiently aligned to make it relatively easy to practice, missing the times when they aren't — which is really when we practice bringing them into alignment. Ad-hoc practice also allows us to ignore our bodies and let our minds or egos drive how we practice. Practicing every day will quickly show us this approach isn't sustainable; we will learn by getting hurt.

This is more challenging in a work environment where we address misalignments between our minds and bodies with coffee, food and other compensating stimulants or pharmaceuticals. Adjusting to bringing them into alignment can be a painful, potentially destructive, and instructive process. The alternative is simple — you may be there already — it is to get sick, sore, fat and/or unhappy.

The question then is how do you bring alignment. Here it is necessary to develop a practice — one that can help surface the gap, and one that when mindfully executed allows for an incremental but fundamental change.
