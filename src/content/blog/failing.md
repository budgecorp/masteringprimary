I would like to call this 'on the edge of failing' however given that failing is less than terminal and therefore at an edge anyways, I say that I am failing in every possible way and have been for an extended period of time.

My work project is on the edge and is at risk of going off the rails. At best it will cost more than we estimated and probably won't be seen as fulfilling its original promise. Beyond my current project my work performance is middle of the road, consumed with busy-ness and little real execution.

I am still overweight, probably 25 pounds. In terms of the more subtle health characteristics I still have dandruff, I'm often tired, my liver and joints are sore. I've gotten much more efficient (i.e. it takes less effort, less fat, less alcohol) at getting to a 'less than healthy' mode.

After 15 years I am nowhere near to developing a solid primary series ashtanga practice. I quickly get frustrated and become focused on obstacles, figuring out solutions requires a lot of effort and a shift in mindset.

I watch more tv/movies than I am happy with. I rarely have adventures and when I do they often revolve around shopping or consuming. I have developed no tangible and measurable skills in many years, perhaps ever? I have not completely committed myself to any goal. Ever.

I am failing to provide my kids with the level of support and engagement that would help make their young lives amazing. I am a mediocre husband. At best.

I'm not engaged in any meaningful way in anything I consider to be interesting. I infrequently and even then in a limited way engage with family and friends. I most of my non-work time resting and reading, with no real intended purpose other than cursory curiosity. The only real emotion I feel is anxiety/fear and it seems to be related to challenges with my liver and unrealized complications with work rather than some real life/death challenge.

I spend most of my time engaged in a career for which I don't think I'm well suited and in which I don't expect to be very successful. The prospect of long term career success is actually demotivating and has been for a while, which probably means my 60-70 hours per week are not being optimally spent.

I haven't done the things I think I'm good at (running, being bold, writing) in any consistent way. Ever.

I find relief, which is clearly just another word for distraction, in spy and crime novels, news, tv and movies. I have not been amazing at anything.

What I have done:

- Gave up Netflix, though I still watch some tv online.
- Largely gave up red meat and dairy.
- I often bring my lunch to work.
- I did a short yoga practice every day for 3 months.
- I've become more aware of some of my less productive habits.
- I've simplified my clothing.
- I've gotten rid of many things that I don't need or use.
- I have avoided buying a second car.
- I got myself a new project at work.
- I've engaged in some work practice activities and I think I've had a positive impact.
- I've learned many new things.
- We've paid off the mortgage and are saving more money.
- I've better understood some of the complexity of living.
- I've read some good books and a couple great books.
- I've spent time with my family.
