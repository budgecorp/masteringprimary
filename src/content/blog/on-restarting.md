I need to keep restarting. I don't know if I am unique in this, but I am supremely skeptical of habits becoming ingrained. An alcoholic can stop drinking, a Christian can stop praying, an Olympian can get fat — so the idea of a 30 or 60 day habit becoming ingrained is a myth.

What I've found true is that doing something every day for 60 days makes it easier to do something else every day for 60 days, or 75, or 4. But having an ingrained habit that you do every day literally requires that you do something every day. Tying that thing to either something you like (feeling lean) or something you don't like (plaque) makes it easier. I can't remember the last time I missed a day of brushing my teeth (I must have been very drunk), but 150 years ago I would have very rarely, if ever, brushed my teeth — so there is nothing inherently natural about it.

I've sometimes been surprised by the things I think are inevitable, like reacting aggressively to someone who is aggressive. I was shocked in my fifties when I started to realize that this was unnecessary and even weak.

Getting started isn't hard. Doing isn't hard. Sticking with it is hard.

To make it less hard:

- **Define a program.** Anyone who physically or mentally impresses you when they are older has a program.
- **Think decades, not years, and definitely not days.** That tight older person has missed some days but not a lot. They lift their weights, run their runs, kept their head in the game. That thing you heard about building a habit in 21 days is not true. It's a habit because you always do it, not just for 21 days. And after 21 days, it is just as easy to quit as it was before 21 days.
- **Remember that not quitting is muscle memory, and this has consequences.** You can do it with things that wreck or things that build. My dad never quit drinking. He also ran most days. The drinking still ended his life early. So select where you build the muscle memory.
- **Will power being limited is a myth.** That person who does something every day forever — they have tremendous will power and you can feel it when you interact with them. It is much stronger than the will power of the person that thinks will power is limited.
- **Food and exercise are foundational.** Trying to be strong in other areas and weak in these will significantly limit you.
- **Faith in relationships** — whether it be god, friends, or family — will make you stronger. Being strong only for yourself will be too limiting.
