There is literally no upside to losing your "cool" at work. This includes:

- When you think something needs to get unstuck
- When you think someone needs to get unstuck
- When you talk calmly but are being aggressive
- When you are interacting with someone who is losing their cool
- When your blood is boiling but others may not see it
- When people who are being kind say they 'couldn't tell'
- When you feel 'passionate' rather than 'frustrated' or 'irritated'
- When your leaders have demonstrated behaviour that is 'way worse'
- When you think you are doing some verbal Aikido
- When you are tired or stressed and think you are just being 'cranky'
- When you are displaying physical signs of upset even if you are not speaking them
- When you are feeling physical signs of upset even if you are not showing them

For each of these you will need to:

- Figure out what you fear or where you have too much pride — it will be one or the other.
- Acknowledge and welcome the feeling as best you can — tough to do in the moment.
- Let that feeling go, if you are willing and to the extent possible, so you can focus on the moment.
- Shift to solution exploration rather than noise.
- This may, and often does, require a step back.

To help:

- Acknowledge that there will always be problems. Try to welcome them as 'life' presenting the appropriate lessons at the appropriate time.
- Recognize that noise is either due to lack of preparation or an inability to stay present and adapt.
- Recognize that assigning fault, to me or others, will always exacerbate the issue and diminish the team's ability to solve problems.

You will be more inclined to lose your cool if:

- You want to be right.
- You aren't getting enough sleep.
- You are ingesting substances that affect your mental state, including caffeine and alcohol — even if it wasn't today (it takes practice to recognize lingering though powerful effects).
- You aren't happy, either at work or in your personal life.
- You feel like life, the situation, or the people involved are treating you unfairly.
- You don't prioritize your relationships.
- You are scared or proud but don't recognize and deal with it.
- You are physically uncomfortable (food, drink, exercise).
