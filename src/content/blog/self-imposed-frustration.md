I am frustrated but think I am doing it to myself. Chipping away at the disciplines that make me happy and then expressing my frustration to feel relevant.

I was not happy today. Felt unbalanced, as if some malevolence — or maybe only difficulty — is building up. Matched with some unwarranted tiredness and an unwillingness to strain, I end up expressing frustration and pushing for others to execute according to my will. Clearly I compensate for my lack of agency by applying my will to others. I did it throughout the day, justified by recent events where I didn't control and the outcomes were embarrassingly, for me, poor.

It leaves me wanting to do other, questioning my worth because I spend it elsewhere than where my heart would take me, and wondering whether I need to push through or pull back. Pushing through brings rot to my breath, a potentially unrecoverable slump to my posture, and revives old head injuries waiting to take on their important role in my future decline.

Now I feel remorse — for the feelings I hurt, the good will I burned, the lack of sophistication I showed in trying to lead the team. The one thing I could do right now to improve the situation is to go to sleep, to rest deeply, and to take on tomorrow with the good will appropriate to the gifts I've been given.

I would like to be smooth and kind, which I seem to only be able to access when I am both caring for and stretching myself to the best of my ability — when I am caring for myself with the discipline that makes me confident in my abilities. If I don't take care of the machine — which includes continuing to test and expand its limits — then I'm unhappy.
