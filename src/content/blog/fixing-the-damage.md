How do you fix the damage you do?

At fifty, you've inevitably accumulated some detritus, through stupidity, fear, anger, lust, laziness or greed. I'd like to think this is true for everyone — it is at least true for me. A few things:

- There is always more.
- Remorse isn't a solution.
- I don't know that you get free without making amends.
- The size of your penance may be inconsistent with your crime.

I've done some bad things. I've thought about one of them for 35 years. I've tried to let it go, I've opened up opportunities to make amends, and I expect it has had a large influence on the person I am today.

It is always sitting there at the bottom of the stack. At the top of a to-do list that is always present, unaddressed — and the log jam behind which I assume there are a backlog of transgressions for me to resolve.

When I practice a lot of yoga it is more present, as if as I strip back the fat, the distractions, I start unveiling the guilt that is lodged in my flesh. It will pass, but I suspect I've only pushed it further to the bone.

Letting go can be a relief but is inevitably ineffective, as is prayer, and I expect — but haven't tried — sharing. It feels like breathing out in a small enclosed room: inevitably I'll breathe it in again.

Resolution requires risk and a willingness to see it through. But, I expect, some transgressions are ours to carry. And that we only resolve them by changing. By becoming a person who would not do as we have done.

I suspect, even though there are probably some immutable laws of right and wrong, that much of the challenge is of our conscience and therefore ours to bear. Or perhaps more accurately, the weight becomes the corrective force that could guide us in our development — to get either closer or further from our chosen entity.
