---

At the end of the week when I am drained it is easy to relate it back to what I ate, slept, and the extent to which I didn't move. If, looking back, I clearly did not control for those variables, I will be drained. If I did control for them, then I will have energy left.

Of course, even though the mathematical equation is simple, the inputs aren't. And they aren't because of our habits, urges, discipline — which all come down to purpose. Are we purpose-driven, and therefore is how we do guided by something more powerful than urges, whims, habits and external triggers?

I think, if we spend our lives driven by anything other than a clearly defined purpose that leaves us enthusiastically energized, then we will naturally be a victim. There is nothing but habits directing us otherwise.

If you are not clearly aware of an overarching purpose that makes you hopeful and energized, you haven't found a reason to be anything other than what comes to you. And in times of challenge you will make the easy choice — which, in my experience, will leave you under-filled and doubtful of your life rather than grateful for it.

As with everything else there is endless complexity here. Practicing gratitude can lead to better feelings and better or more proactive choices, and going with the flow can be a positive experience. In the second case this will only be true if you are conscious and not burdened with baggage that means we trick ourselves into thinking we know when we are actually in the flow.

In the yoga world this would relate to brahmacharya and ahimsa.
