title: 'Mental Toughness Fundamentals'
description: 'Ten principles I keep coming back to — define the win, control the state, prioritize the priorities.'
pubDate: 2026-05-03
draft: true
---

**1. Define my win.** What am I no-holds committed to? Are my priorities and purpose clear? Without this I will find it impossible to engage with passion and inspiration. The best I can hope for is to be capable. Need more than a willingness to work hard and a desire not to fail if I'm going to be happy with who I am in the world.

The Buddhists say I can learn to be happy with who I am regardless of how I am doing and what I am doing. I think this is only true in regards to how the world interacts with me, not in how I engage with my life. I very much doubt I'll find fulfillment, especially on a deathbed, by learning to primarily accept. Yoga seems to be firmly situated in the realm of thoughtful, effortful and consistent effort. An insufficient amount of purposeful effort will, I think, always leave me unhappy.

Therefore — what am I, balls-down, committed to doing? I cannot think of a single thing I've accomplished that was the fruit of an all-in commitment. I ran a marathon, biked to Halifax, got a job in consulting because I started and ended up there. I didn't make an all-in commitment.

**2. Create my self-image vision.** 30 seconds every day to visualize who I want to be, how I want my life to be. The thought being that creating the vision will help make it happen. I will need to be specific in the details. Who do I want to be in 2 or 5 years? The vision needs to be sufficient to make me excited and passionate.

Can I create a vision that excites me?

**3. Set product goals and emphasize process goals.** Make sure there is a clear path from "wanting" to actually "accomplishing". This is about doing what it takes all the way, all the time.

**4. Prioritize the priorities.** Prioritize the process goals, every day. Schedule time to ensure that this happens.

**5. Complete daily performance evaluations.** Daily and quarterly evaluation is necessary.

**6. Control my state.** 1) Will the situation get better by having an emotional reaction? If not then... 2) What am I trying to avoid by having an emotional reaction?

**7. Know your scripts.** Create and memorize scripts for key interactions.

**8. Prepare mentally every day.** Complete the 100-second workout every day. This is 1) focusing breath 2) identity statement 3) vision highlight reel 4) identity statement 5) focusing breath.

**9. Practice relentless solution focus.** Recognizing and resolving problems is one of my primary jobs. This involves replacing negative thinking with hope and confidence by relentlessly asking "What is the one thing I can do differently to make this better?" It is helpful to remember and internalize that "Every problem has the seed of equal or greater benefit, if approached with a positive mental attitude." This will take lots of practice.

**10. Adopt Gable discipline.** Do what you set your mind to, all the way and every time.
