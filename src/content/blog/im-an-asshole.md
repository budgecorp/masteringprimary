This isn't something that dawned on me one morning. It is a realization that is made clearer to me repeatedly throughout the day. Sometimes it comes unbidden with a hot flush followed by a recent or distant memory; I find it odd when the flush precedes the memory, as if some deeper conscience is processing old memory and bringing it to my attention when it is sufficiently shameful. Sometimes the realization occurs when I reflect on something I said or did a minute or 5 seconds ago and I don't like the person who could have said or done that thing. Sometimes the realization occurs when I realize something I'm going to say or do is something that an asshole would say or do; this last one is tricky in the course of a business conversation since I then trip as I try to course correct, making it clear to anyone paying attention that not only am I inclined to be an asshole but I'm also incompetent — since I either can't sufficiently mask or accept this part of my nature to make it palatable. Sometimes the realization comes when I'm woken up by an overwhelming sense of remorse, often with the remnants of a very clear dream to illustrate the issue.

I hope this is the yoga. It is scary to think that I am becoming an asshole. I'd much prefer to accept that I am an asshole and yoga is helping me see the larger number of instances in which this is true. I, of course, feel remorse, but to be honest, it feels overwhelmingly persistent.

I think it is helpful to break down the ways I'm an asshole in order to be able to better process them.

- The ways I have made excuses for my lack of engagement in the things I purport to be important to me.
- The ways I have been mean or at least deficient with my kids.
- Watching me try to be better than those around me.
- The ways I have been mean to my wife.
- The ways I've been mean to the other people I care about.
- All the things I don't do well, when I probably could.
- My thin strategy for masking my ineptitude is seen through.
- The little shitty things I say and do to be right or smart or — well, mostly right.
- The ignorance allows me to act as if I have all the time in the world when it is pretty obvious that's not the case.

Breaking them down allows me to see that they aren't that complex and generally come down to one thing: my need to protect myself from the reality of my ineptitude.

An example comes to mind. My wife and I were crossing a street in downtown Toronto and a large black Bentley pulled in front of us blocking our path, and I reacted with a gesture of exasperation which provoked the extra-muscled man in the front seat to roll down his window and lift an eyebrow. My point is the very expensive car and large muscle, both of which were pretty overwhelmingly impressive, didn't reduce the degree to which he was acting like an asshole. Regardless of how satisfying these were in the moment, he would probably find them to be limiting at some point in the future. In my experience a crying baby is a pretty good leveller of the playing field but I expect there are many other examples — including long-term debilitating illness or the loss of a loved one.

I'm hoping the way is through, but this is absolutely not clear to me yet. I can temporarily manage the remorse through meditation, exercise, hard work or better communication, however I think it is ultimately necessary to sufficiently feel the remorse so that it changes me. I just hope it doesn't change me into a bitter, self-critical, scared and inflexible version of myself. This seems like a definite possibility, and a persistently consistent dedicated practice seems the only viable alternative.

Maybe this is the point of the practice.
