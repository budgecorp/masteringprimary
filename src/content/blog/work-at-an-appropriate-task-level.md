---
title: 'Work at an Appropriate Task Level'
description: 'Doing lower-level work feels useful. It builds the wrong habits.'
pubDate: 2026-05-03
draft: true
---

Sometimes we do work that is at a lower level than our team thinks we should be operating. We want to perceive this as useful because:

1. It is work we know how to do and therefore can do well.
2. We are helping the team make progress.

However, doing lower-level work builds the wrong habits. The right habit is for team members to deal with the tasks that are in their part of the machine. If jumping into someone else's part of the machine, it must be done:

1. With discrimination (only when necessary).
2. With clear agreement.
3. As quickly as possible and consistent with the agreement.
4. Once completed, it is necessary to figure out a permanent fix.

To do otherwise will:

1. Erode the team's sense of responsibility.
2. Encourage a habit of control rather than empowerment.
3. Provide a personal illusion of productivity, since the progress is happening at the wrong level.
4. Result in gaps at the personal level of responsibility.

This principle is about doing the work rather than fixing the machine. Fixing the machine is one of the key responsibilities of leadership.
