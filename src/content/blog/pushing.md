I find myself often pushing when there is no real purpose, as if the pushing somehow justifies itself. In retrospect it is most often an approach that allows me not to figure out how to do something properly.

I push my son to kick the ball better rather than to take the time over weeks or months to help him learn how to kick a ball. I push a colleague to justify project pricing rather than taking the time to understand what he has put together and why. I push my boss to justify his approach rather than accept and adjust to his approach. I push myself in a posture rather than accept and work within the current state my body or mind is at that particular point.

The lesson seems to be the same:

- Completely accept where you are at. It is very easy to 'accept' however if at the next moment I am gritting my teeth and pushing again, I know I haven't really accepted.
- Regularly, with a great attitude and complete commitment, work at improving in the areas that are important.
- Identify and watch for the signs that I am pushing. Some of these are easy — I'm impatient or interrupting people — but some of them require much more subtlety.

The practice is also fairly common, regardless of the use case:

- Find a tool to counteract the inclination to push. I find that consciously relaxing the roof of my mouth or the skin on the back of my neck works (an Iyengar suggestion).
- Become sensitive to resistance — our own and that of others — and soften into it while maintaining posture. I find this especially tricky with kids, where I distrust the resistance and label it as laziness. From a yoga perspective this means standing at the front of your mat, recognizing and accepting the resistance to start, and then starting the practice paying very close attention to the resistance. Ignoring the resistance, in all instances, will lead to injury, of some kind.
