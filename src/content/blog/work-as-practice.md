title: 'What If Work Was a Practice?'

---

Most of us, even if we engage in a regular effort to increase our awareness and to clarify our moment-by-moment intention, go through the work day in reaction and usually in a manner inconsistent with our values, to the extent that they are articulated or known.

Across all modes, levels, and complexity of work we find people who are clear about the core values of their work and are positively engaged in working consistent with those values. What would it mean if we treated our work as an intentional practice?

This is consistent with much of the self-help literature of the last 80 years and consistent with the idea that we engage in a rigorous practice to help engrain the values that underlie that practice in how we operate in the rest of our lives.

If we used yoga as an example — recognizing that any rigorous practice done for a long time in a consistent manner with full enthusiasm would also apply — this would mean:

- Dedicate the effort to something greater than us.
- Do it consistently, with full vigour, for a long time.
- Be crystal clear on intention.
- Be crystal clear on values, and stick to those values regardless of the situation.
- Pay attention to what distracts and keep coming back to the practice. This is obviously more complicated than moving through a series of postures; clarity is critical.
- Acknowledge where you are at and start from there.
- Bring a mental and physical firmness or posture; when you can't maintain it any longer, recognize the limit and figure out how to work with this edge.
- Formally mark the beginning and end, ideally with a ritual that reinforces intention and purpose.
- Recognize what gets in the way: food, drink, mental constructs, conflicting habits, lack of sleep.
