Many of us in the developed world are interested in maintaining a body weight that is different than what we are currently carrying around. Like many of these people I have tried to change my body weight through fitness, diet, positive thinking, supportive peers, blah, blah, blah, without any permanent success. In retrospect it is more likely that I have developed a deficiency mindset of the 'you get what you think about' variety, and my attention to being overweight likely resulted in more barriers than intended results.

A little background: I grew up skinny, added a little muscle in high school and university, ended my twenties pretty fit and did ok until I hit my mid-30s, kids, and lack of sleep. I topped out about 245 pounds and 28% body fat.

I was 240 by the time the family headed to Mysore India and our commitment to Ashtanga. Three months later I'd lost 40 lbs. Diet likely had an impact but I did not consciously manage my diet in India. I also only got sick a couple of times for less than 24 hours. I expect the two main drivers behind my weight loss were a daily 1.25 hour Ashtanga practice and a significant reduction in stress (mostly accomplished by taking a lot of naps).

I have found in the past that I can quickly lose some weight by taking a holiday and napping a lot — however this usually means a few pounds, and I will therefore focus here on the impact of Ashtanga.

In the moderate temperature of an Indian morning my practice results in a couple cups of sweat. My take on this is that I retain a lot of water to buffer my body from the poisons it contains. I therefore have a lot of water available to sweat out. Long-time practitioners do not seem to sweat as much as me, and I hope this state will change. In the meantime I must have lost a lot of water weight, hopefully expelling some 'poisons' from my system at the same time.

My digestion was regular but felt inefficient in expelling all that it needed to. This was most apparent in the difference in my ability to move my belly after a few months. When starting it felt like an undifferentiated bulk; after a few months I was aware of my internal belly muscles and structures and able to move them independently of each other.

I lost a significant amount of body fat. At 28% body fat (using the calliper method) and 240 pounds I had about 67 lbs of fat on my body. After 6 months I was 190 and 12% body fat — about 23 lbs of fat. I added a significant amount of muscle in the process.
