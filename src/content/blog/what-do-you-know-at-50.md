Let's assume by the time you hit fifty you understand how to play the game. You've known since you were 10 that playing well with others bears fruit, that eating right is not complicated, that addictions are to be avoided or worked out, that loving someone is necessary for happiness, that exercising till you sweat must happen everyday, and that we need to consciously manage ourselves do the right thing.

And yet, even though we've understood these fundamentals for 40+ years, we still don't do them. Some people engage in a course of betterment. Most bury themselves in the dirt, the inevitable result of a lack of intent and effot, of their choices (food, drink, anger, resentment, entertainment, greed) till it becomes an integral part of who they are.

So let's set a standard that most of us probably aspire to: I have key behaviours related to body, mind, spirit, and relationship on auto-pilot, and I know how to pull out of a dive, with some grace, when auto-pilot fails.

Let's acknowledge that most of us are failing to adhere to this standard, and that auto-pilot periods are brief and the tailspins can last decades.

Further, let's accept that we will die unexplored and disappointed if we don't find a different path.

And, so, let's correct.

I have the same qualifications to do this as you. I've tried, I've aspired, I've been complacent, I thought I'd be helped, I've done some things well, I want more.

There are an endless paths and I will choose the yogic path — because I like the established and thoughtful culture  I've appreciated many of its adherents, and mostly because it is thousands of years of practice formalized in a method, unencumbered, if you choose, by dogma.
