Let's assume by the time you hit fifty you understand how to play the game. You've known since you were 10 that playing well with others bears fruit, that eating right is not complicated, that addictions are to be avoided or figured out, that loving someone is critical to happiness, that exercise is necessary, and that we need to help ourselves do the right thing.

And yet, even though we've understood these fundamentals for 40+ years, we still don't do them. Some people, like Ben Franklin, engage in a course of betterment. Most bury themselves in the dirt of their choice (food, drink, anger, resentment, entertainment, religion) well before they are dead.

So let's set a standard that most of us probably aspire to: I have key behaviours related to body, mind, spirit, and relationship on auto-pilot, and I know how to pull out of a dive, with some grace, when auto-pilot fails.

Let's acknowledge that most of us are failing miserably to adhere to this standard, and that auto-pilot periods are brief and the tailspins go on for decades.

Further, let's accept that we will die with tremendous regret if we don't resolve our shortcomings.

And, let's move quickly to correct.

I have the same qualifications to do this as you. I've tried, I've aspired, I've been complacent, I've achieved, I want more.

There are an endless number of paths and I will choose the yogic path — partly because I like the inherent culture and I've appreciated many of its adherents, but mostly because it is thousands of years of practice formalized in a method, unencumbered, if you choose, by dogma.
