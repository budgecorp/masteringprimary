I am often 'stuck'. I use the quotes because I have so many options to become 'unstuck', including remembering that being stuck is just another choice. Yoga offers that the objective is more profound than just being unstuck — it is liberation, defined as stilling the fluctuations of the mind, a profound outcome that is only recognized in the touching of it.

Like so many problems then, the shortest route to getting unstuck is to recognize that the problem is not relevant — there is something more important to solve for.

'Your client doesn't like you' becomes 'how can I best serve the organization my client works for'. 'My code doesn't work' becomes 'what am I really trying to accomplish, and have I thought through how best to do it'. 'My knee hurts' becomes 'how can I strengthen my knees so I can run a really fast 10k'. 'I'm never going to do padmasana' becomes 'what can I do right now to loosen my left hip and get deeper into this posture'.

So often the best practical answer to feeling stuck is to ask, as suggested by Jason Selk: "What is the one thing I can do right now to make this a little better?" As you can see, this naturally reframes in a positive, actionable manner that, over time, builds confidence in your ability to adapt, solve challenges, engage with energy, and make things a little bit better.

This habit — and it should be everyone's habit — can be continuously practiced in yoga, where the obstacles are uncountable, always changing, and ultimately will become one's allies. Yoga is practice, not an outcome, and that practice becomes steady — the primary goal — only when done for a long time, continuously, and with earnest devotion.
